# One course for two requirements: what each program means

MAINT 5.18, step 1. Research only: no database write, no code change. Checked 26 October 2026.

## The answer in short

The 53 programs of 10 October are 51 now: TUM's two were rewritten under the German model (CONTENT 7,
Part B1, 12 October) and no longer list a course twice. Of the 51:

| Kind | Programs | Today's result | What the fix needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Different subjects | 4: Manchester MBChB Medicine, UCL BSc Psychology, Trinity Medicine, Trinity Dental Science | Wrong: one science meets both requirements | Mark the two science requirements as each needing its own course |
| B. Different subjects, stored once | 14: Trinity's 13 science strands and Physiotherapy | Wrong the other way round: "two of" is stored as one requirement, so one science is enough | Add the second requirement, then mark both |
| C. One course may count twice | 33: Lausanne 15, Edinburgh 12, NTU 2, Basel, Western, Manchester MPharm, Trinity Pharmacy | Right | Nothing |

No row is a data error in the sense of the task (rows that contradict the page). Kind B is incomplete
rather than wrong: the content refresh stored one "two of" requirement on purpose and said so in each
program's notes, because the matcher could not hold two.

**For the owner (step 2): store the rule on the requirement, not on the program.** A program-wide
switch ("every requirement needs its own course") would refuse students the universities accept:

- **Trinity Medicine and Dental Science** need Biology, Chemistry or Physics at HL 6 and another of them
  at HL 5, and "if you do not have a qualification in Physics", Maths SL 5. Physics at HL meets a
  science requirement and the physics one at once. And a student with no Physics needs Maths for both
  the physics-or-maths requirement and the minimum maths row (SL 4). With the program-wide switch, a
  student with Biology HL 6, Chemistry HL 5 and Maths SL 5 fails, though Trinity accepts them.
- **Trinity's science strands, once their second requirement is added (kind B)**, ask for Maths SL 5
  and two HL 5s from a list that includes Maths. Maths HL 5 counts as the maths requirement and as one
  of the two. With the program-wide switch, Maths HL 5 and Biology HL 5 would fail; Trinity accepts them.

The alternative is one boolean column on `ProgramCourseRequirement`, off by default: "needs a course of
its own". Within a program, the requirements that have it must each be met by a different course. The
ones without it may use any course, as today. Every row of an either/or group carries the same value.
That covers all 18 programs of kinds A and B and every "two of" rule found below. No program needs two
separate sets of exclusive requirements, so a set number is not needed. A program with no marked
requirement takes today's code path unchanged, which keeps the fingerprint byte-identical. The column
adds one value per requirement row to the programs cache (about 3 MB today), so leave it out when false.

## How this was found

- The list: `ProgramCourseRequirement` grouped by program and course, kept where a course is in more
  than one `COALESCE("orGroupId", id)`, joined to the program, university and course names (632 rows,
  ids and names only). Read-only, through `DIRECT_URL`.
- Each program's stored notes from `scripts/programs/2027/<university>.ts`, which quote the official
  pages read in CONTENT 3.4 to 7.
- The official pages, fetched on 26 October 2026. Edinburgh, Manchester, NTU, Western, Basel,
  swissuniversities, UNIL and Trinity answer curl. UCL does not (403 to scripts), so its page was read
  from the Internet Archive.
- The 10 October measurement (1,159 profile and program pairs) counted every shared course as a
  change. Most of it was kind C, where today's result is right: Lausanne's 615 pairs, Edinburgh's 134,
  Basel's 38, NTU's 24, Western's 3 and Manchester MPharm's share. The real change is in kinds A and B.
  How many pairs that is will be known once step 3 can re-run the count with the flags set.
